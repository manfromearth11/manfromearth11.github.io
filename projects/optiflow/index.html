OptiFlow

Learning Multimodal One-step Flow Policy

01 / OVERVIEW

OptiFlow learns an expressive one-step policy for offline reinforcement learning through value-weighted optimal transport. Our contributions are:

  • Policy improvement with behavior-supported targets. The reference policy supplies candidate actions, and critic values determine their priority. The one-step policy learns from these targets without direct critic maximization.
  • Preserving multiple high-value modes. Transport constraints allocate mass across good actions, helping retain distinct modes even when the dataset represents them unevenly.
  • One-step inference with strong empirical performance. All transport computation stays in training. The deployed policy takes a single forward pass, with gains over FQL and GFP on aggregate OGBench and D4RL AntMaze benchmarks.

02 / THE CHALLENGE

In-support, high-value, expressive—and speed

We aim to address four requirements for offline RL:

  • In-support. Choose actions supported by the offline data, where value estimates are more reliable.
  • High-value. Favor good actions rather than simply reproduce the dataset, which may also contain suboptimal behavior.
  • Expressive. Represent multiple distinct good actions without collapsing to a single mode or averaging between them.
  • Speed. Generate an action in a single forward pass, avoiding iterative sampling at deployment.

Can we preserve distinct, high-value modes within behavior support—and generate actions in one step?

03 / OUR APPROACH

  1. Learn the reference policy and critic

    The reference policy proposes actions learned from offline data. The critic estimates their values.

  2. Match with value-weighted optimal transport

    Higher-value reference actions receive more weight. Optimal transport uses these weights and action distances to assign targets to one-step samples, preserving distinct high-value modes.

  3. Distill into a one-step policy

    Train the one-step policy on its assigned targets. At inference, generate an action in one forward pass.

04 / ALGORITHM

  • OptiFlow has three components: a critic Q_\phi, a reference flow policy \mu_\omega, and a one-step policy \mu_\theta.
  • The critic is trained with the standard TD loss.
  • The reference policy is trained with value-aware behavior cloning (VaBC):
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(\omega)=\mathbb E\left[\underbrace{g_\eta(s,a)}_{\text{Value weight}}\underbrace{\left\|v_\omega(s,a_t,t)-(a-\epsilon)\right\|_2^2}_{\text{Flow-matching loss}}\right]
    Here, a is a dataset action, \epsilon is Gaussian noise, and a_t = (1-t)\epsilon + ta. The weight g_\eta favors higher-value dataset actions.
  • The one-step policy learns from reference actions assigned by value-weighted optimal transport:
    How does value-weighted optimal transport work?

    For each state, sample N one-step actions a_{\theta,i}^{s} and M reference actions \tilde a_{\omega,j}^{s}. Give every one-step sample equal mass and prioritize reference actions using their critic values:

    p_i=\frac{1}{N},\qquad q_j^{s}=\frac{\exp(Q_\phi(s,\tilde a_{\omega,j}^{s})/\tau)}{\sum_{k=1}^{M}\exp(Q_\phi(s,\tilde a_{\omega,k}^{s})/\tau)}

    Value determines the reference mass. Geometry determines the matching cost, using squared action distances normalized by their per-state mean \bar c^{s}:

    C_{ij}^{s}=\frac{\|a_{\theta,i}^{s}-\tilde a_{\omega,j}^{s}\|_2^2}{\bar c^{s}}

    Sinkhorn computes an entropy-regularized transport plan with these row and column marginals:

    P^{s,*}=\arg\min_{P\geq0}\left[\langle P,C^{s}\rangle+\varepsilon\sum_{i,j}P_{ij}(\log P_{ij}-1)\right],\qquad P\mathbf1=p,\quad P^\top\mathbf1=q^{s}

    Each one-step sample then takes the reference action with the largest mass in its row as its target. That transport mass also weights the distillation update below.

    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{distill}}(\theta)=\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\underbrace{P^{s,*}_{i,j_i^*}}_{\text{Transport weight}}\underbrace{\left\|\mu_\theta(s,z_i)-\tilde a_{\omega,j_i^*}^{s}\right\|_2^2}_{\text{Distillation loss}}\right],\qquad j_i^*\in\arg\max_jP^{s,*}_{ij}
    P^{s,*} is the value-weighted OT plan for state s. For each one-step sample i, j_i^* selects the reference action with the largest transport mass in row i. The selected action \tilde a_{\omega,j_i^*}^{s} is its distillation target. The plan and targets are fixed during the update. There is no direct critic maximization. This distillation is the same in OptiFlow and OptiFlow-BC.
  • The output is the one-step policy: a single forward pass at inference.

05 / CONTROLLED EXPERIMENTS

Two-mode bandit

Two high-value modes are separated by a suboptimal region. Can a policy capture both modes while staying within behavior support?

Flow Q-Learning (FQL) trains a one-step policy by balancing Q maximization and reference distillation:

\underbrace{\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D,\,a^\pi\sim\pi_\theta}\!\left[-Q_\phi(s,a^\pi)\right]}_{\text{Q loss}} + \alpha\cdot \underbrace{\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D,\,z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)}\!\left[\left\|\mu_\theta(s,z)-\mu_\omega(s,z)\right\|_2^2\right]}_{\text{Distillation loss}}
\begin{aligned}&\underbrace{\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D,\,a^\pi\sim\pi_\theta}\!\left[-Q_\phi(s,a^\pi)\right]}_{\text{Q loss}}\\[12pt]+{}&\alpha\cdot \underbrace{\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D,\,z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)}\!\left[\left\|\mu_\theta(s,z)-\mu_\omega(s,z)\right\|_2^2\right]}_{\text{Distillation loss}}\end{aligned}

Large \alpha emphasizes distillation and can pull actions into the suboptimal band between modes. Small \alpha emphasizes Q-maximization and can push actions outside behavior support.

Four equally optimal goals

The four goals are equally optimal, but unevenly represented in the dataset. FQL trades off diversity and quality as its distillation strength changes: stronger distillation improves mode coverage but reduces return. OptiFlow achieves balanced coverage of all four goals with the highest return in this experiment.

This experiment uses a standard BC-trained reference policy to isolate the effect of OT distillation from value-aware reference learning.

Performance across navigation and manipulation

What does transport contribute?

On the same 10-task OGBench ablation suite, FQL and OptiFlow-BC use the same standard BC reference setup. Changing the distillation mechanism raises average one-step success from 37.6% to 48.6%. Adding the value-aware reference in full OptiFlow raises it further to 57.3%. Better target allocation and better candidate proposals contribute separately.
